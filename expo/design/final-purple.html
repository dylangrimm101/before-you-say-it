6a Before You Say It · FINAL PURPLE · Rork handoff · locked visual direction

Frosted layers on a cool luminous field, with a single purple gradient carrying the one thing to do today.

This is the locked visual direction. The earlier peach and slate explorations are superseded — nothing in this document carries them forward. This pass is implementation readiness only: contrast, privacy copy, button geometry, demo-data consistency, keyboard states, production icons, dynamic-content annotations and overflow behaviour. No curriculum, product-strategy, flow or scoring changes. Depth carries the hierarchy: a cool near-white gradient field behind everything, cooling into violet where the light falls, translucent frosted cards at 28 pt radius that sit a measurable distance off it, and one saturated gradient surface per screen holding the single action that matters. Everything less important recedes — lower opacity, softer shadow, further back. One geometric sans (Plus Jakarta Sans) across the product; weight does the rest. Type scale rationalized 12 sizes → 6. Horizontal padding unified at 22 pt. Card borders removed wherever type hierarchy can do the grouping. The gradient surface appears at most once per screen, and it is always the thing to do next. Frames are 393 × 852 with 59 pt top and 34 pt bottom safe areas, laid out in flexbox only — no CSS grid and no hover-dependent states. Translucency is deliberate and load-bearing here: every card is a real glass layer over the gradient, so depth reads without borders. Program is always the 30-Day Conversation Practice; the word “challenge” is gone.

Button geometry is now consistent with the component system: primary and ghost buttons 18 pt radius, chips and category filters pills, the mic circular, and a pill CTA only inside the HeroSurface where it is intentional. Small type has been darkened to #646D77 / #5B646E for AA on the pale field.

Icons · dynamic content · overflow · platform chrome
Production icons All glyphs are named SF Symbols equivalents drawn at 1.7 px stroke. Visual size 22–26 pt inside a 44 pt minimum tap target; the mic control is 88 pt visual in a 104 pt target.

Every icon-only control carries the accessibility label above; none rely on the glyph alone. The 9:41 status bar and the 59 pt / 34 pt insets in these frames are illustrative only. Overflow & accessibility
{{ ov.case }} {{ ov.rule }}
Keyboard behaviour Two keyboard frames are in section 3. Rules for both: Fixed — the top bar and step progress stay pinned; the primary CTA rides directly above the keyboard on the safe-area inset, never behind it. Scrolls — only the question and the text field. When the keyboard opens, the field scrolls to sit just above the accessory row; the heading may scroll off. Dismiss — “Done” in the accessory row, a tap outside the field, or a downward drag on the scroll area. Return inserts a newline; it never submits. Dictation and the keyboard are mutually exclusive — opening one closes the other. Composing — the transcript field is the same component as Setup step 1. Send it stays enabled with edited text; Say it again discards the edit and returns to Listening.
Privacy language — must match architecture The privacy copy in these frames is a design intent, not a legal statement. Before implementation, audio capture, transcription, storage location, retention period and deletion behaviour must all be confirmed against the built architecture and the copy updated to match — including whether transcripts are device-local, account-scoped, or both. Conflicting claims have been removed from this document: transcripts are described as kept with the account until deleted, and no audio recordings are retained. If the implementation differs, this is the copy that changes. “Your practice isn’t shared with the person you’re rehearsing about” replaces every broad “nothing is shared” claim. Do not reintroduce absolute statements the backend cannot guarantee.
